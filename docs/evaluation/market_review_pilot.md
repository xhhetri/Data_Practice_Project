# Assessment 4: recurring fuel-market review pilot

Status: prospective protocol; no recruited participants, observed savings or adoption results are claimed.

Recruit three to five reachable industry/energy analysts who actually prepare a fuel-market review. Start with their last real briefing: audience, information sources, time required, decisions supported and pain points. Students can test interface comprehension separately, but their participation is not evidence of professional demand.

For an initial matched-task comparison, freeze the same price, stock and sales evidence in both conditions. Counterbalance FuelScope and the participant's existing source/report workflow. Ask each participant to identify dated wholesale-price changes, distinguish national stocks from state sales, report the monthly model and baseline errors, and prepare a short cited review. Record completion time, factual errors, interpretation errors and assistance. Compare against existing AIP/ACCC/official reports where those are their normal sources, not an artificially inefficient baseline.

Then run four actual weekly review cycles. Observe meaningful new information, whether the participant voluntarily returns, why they return or stop, which evidence enters a real briefing, and what action it supports. Record unchanged weeks and failed/stale feeds. A saved checkpoint or mandatory student visit is not adoption evidence.

Report anonymised paired completion times, errors and source traceability separately from voluntary return and decision usefulness. Explain the small sample, order effects and task differences. No causal policy effect, fuel savings or shortage avoidance may be inferred from these tasks.
